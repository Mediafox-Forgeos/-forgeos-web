# User Decision Matrix

**Work order:** WO-ARGOS-032 (Product Reality Check)
**Status:** PRODUCT VALIDATION. No code, API, migration, or `schema.prisma` change. No new screens — this document evaluates the four real screens WO-ARGOS-031 already shipped (`/command-center`, `/network`, `/operations`, `/analytics`, PR #51 — see [KYLUM_CONSOLE_VISUAL_GUIDE.md](./KYLUM_CONSOLE_VISUAL_GUIDE.md) for exactly what's built).
**Mission:** for every screen — who opens it, when, how often, what decision it produces, what a wrong answer costs, and what disappears if the screen is removed. The question this document exists to answer is not "does this screen look good" but "does someone need it."

## Command Center

- **Who opens it:** the day-to-day operations champion ([ICP_AND_BUYER_PERSONAS.md](../commercial/ICP_AND_BUYER_PERSONAS.md)) — the person who logs into MOVOS at all, not a specialist role.
- **When:** first thing at login, every session — it's the landing screen, not a choice.
- **How often:** at minimum once a day; realistically re-checked 2–5 times a day as a reflex ("is anything on fire") rather than a deliberate workflow step.
- **What decision is taken:** a single triage call — _keep doing whatever I was already doing, or drop it and go investigate._ Nothing more specific than that. Command Center never tells the operator what to do next in detail; it tells them whether to go looking.
- **What happens if the information is wrong:** two distinct failure modes, both real risks given [LEARNING_METRICS.md](./LEARNING_METRICS.md)'s own finding that MOVOS cannot yet measure false-positive rate. A false "healthy" verdict (a real problem the precedence logic or the underlying data doesn't yet surface) delays response to something already costing revenue or driver trust. A false "attention needed" verdict wastes the operator's attention and, repeated enough times, teaches them to stop trusting the badge at all — the same "boy who cried wolf" risk any alerting surface faces once its accuracy is unproven.
- **What happens if the screen doesn't exist:** less than it might seem. Every fact on Command Center already exists somewhere else in the console — station connectivity is also on Network, open-action count is also the Operations column headers, occupancy is also on Network and Analytics. Removing Command Center would not remove any information from MOVOS; it would remove the one-glance aggregation of it. That is a real, defensible value (see [WIDGET_VALUE_ANALYSIS.md](./WIDGET_VALUE_ANALYSIS.md)'s duplication findings), but it is a speed/convenience value, not an exclusive-data value — an honest distinction worth naming plainly rather than overstating.

## Network

- **Who opens it:** the same operations champion, usually _after_ Command Center sent them here — this is rarely the first screen of a session.
- **When:** reactively, when the health verdict reads non-healthy (Flow C, [KYLUM_CONSOLE_USER_FLOWS.md](./KYLUM_CONSOLE_USER_FLOWS.md)), or as a periodic manual sweep independent of any alert.
- **How often:** variable and event-driven — zero times on a calm day, several times on a bad one. Less predictable in frequency than Command Center, but higher-stakes per visit.
- **What decision is taken:** _which specific station, and what kind of problem._ This is a diagnostic decision, not a triage one — it requires identity (which station) and a first read on cause (connectivity vs. hardware fault), which Command Center's aggregate counts cannot provide.
- **What happens if the information is wrong:** a real, physical-world cost. `StationHealthService.computeHealth()`'s precedence rule (connectivity evidence before fault evidence) means a station that is both offline _and_ has a faulted connector reads simply as "offline" — the fault is invisible until connectivity is restored. An operator dispatching on this screen's information alone could send the wrong kind of response (a network technician instead of a hardware technician), a real wasted truck-roll, or worse, believe a station is fine on reconnection when it's actually still faulted underneath.
- **What happens if the screen doesn't exist:** a real regression, not a convenience loss. Nothing else in the console shows individual station identity with drill-down health — Command Center's cards are aggregate counts only. Without Network, an operator alerted to a problem would have no way to find out _where_ it is inside the product at all.

## Operations

- **Who opens it:** the operations champion, and — once real technician/dispatch roles exist (see [PRODUCT_GAPS.md](./PRODUCT_GAPS.md)) — whoever actually works a case day to day.
- **When:** continuously through the day, not tied to a single trigger — this is the screen an active operator plausibly keeps open in a second tab rather than visiting and leaving.
- **How often:** highest interaction _count_ of the four screens, even if not the highest _visit_ count — every acknowledge/assign/snooze/resolve/dismiss transition happens here, and it's the only screen in the console with write actions at all.
- **What decision is taken:** _what do I personally do about this specific problem, right now, and is it closed._ The only screen that produces a durable, attributable outcome (an `Action` state transition) rather than just information.
- **What happens if the information is wrong:** direct operational cost. A severity misjudged (a `HIGH` treated as routine) means a real problem sits too long. A case marked `RESOLVED` when the underlying condition wasn't actually fixed produces a false sense of closure — and, per [LEARNING_METRICS.md](./LEARNING_METRICS.md)'s recurrence-rate metric, is exactly the pattern that metric exists to catch, once it's actually being tracked.
- **What happens if the screen doesn't exist:** the most severe regression of the four. Without it, MOVOS reverts to WO-ARGOS-025's read-only Recommendation Engine — the exact state [OPERATIONAL_INTELLIGENCE_TECHNICAL_NOTES.md](../implementation/OPERATIONAL_INTELLIGENCE_TECHNICAL_NOTES.md) already named as an explicit limitation ("no acknowledge/dismiss action") before WO-ARGOS-026 fixed it. This isn't a hypothetical loss; it's the specific gap the Action Center was built to close, still the console's only real workflow engine.

## Analytics

- **Who opens it:** here the persona genuinely shifts. Nothing on this screen supports a same-day operational decision — its content (trends, ranking, aggregate energy) matches [ICP_AND_BUYER_PERSONAS.md](../commercial/ICP_AND_BUYER_PERSONAS.md)'s **economic buyer** persona far better than the day-to-day champion who lives on the other three screens.
- **When:** not daily. Periodic — weekly, or ad hoc when someone specifically asks "how are we doing" (internally, or ahead of a renewal/expansion conversation, per [COMMERCIAL_JOURNEY_MAP.md](../commercial/COMMERCIAL_JOURNEY_MAP.md) Flow D).
- **How often:** lowest of the four screens, by a wide margin — this is the honest finding this document is built to surface.
- **What decision is taken:** _is this network worth continued investment, and is it trending the right way_ — a strategic/reporting decision, not an operational one. Nothing on this screen tells anyone what to do today.
- **What happens if the information is wrong:** lower daily operational risk than the other three (nobody dispatches a technician off an Analytics number), but real business risk over a longer horizon — a wrong trend read could support a bad expansion decision, or a false confidence going into a renewal conversation. Compounded by two real, already-documented sampling limits: the 200-session cap on `GET /sessions` (no date-range filter exists) and the complete absence of revenue (CAP-010 not built, out of scope by this work order's own predecessor).
- **What happens if the screen doesn't exist:** the least _immediately_ missed of the four in daily use — nobody's day breaks without it. But it is the only place a growth-trend question can be answered at all, which matters for exactly the commercial conversations [PRICING_AND_PACKAGING.md](../commercial/PRICING_AND_PACKAGING.md) and [COMMERCIAL_JOURNEY_MAP.md](../commercial/COMMERCIAL_JOURNEY_MAP.md) describe. The honest conclusion is not "cut it" — it's that Analytics is serving a different job, for a different person, on a different cadence than the other three, and should not be judged by the same "does the operator need this every morning" bar. [WIDGET_VALUE_ANALYSIS.md](./WIDGET_VALUE_ANALYSIS.md) and the success-criteria answer in this sprint's cover discussion return to this distinction directly.
